# Piece-Aware Board Localization and Calibration Specification

**Version:** 0.2  
**Status:** Milestone 1 implemented; Milestone 2 partially implemented;
Milestones 3-5 proposed
**Platform:** Python on Windows  
**Related specifications:**

- [Projector/camera chessboard system](./projector-camera-chessboard-specification.md)
- [Projector calibration](./projector-calibration-specification.md)
- [Single-shot coded-pattern calibration](./single-shot-coded-pattern-calibration-specification.md)

## 1. Purpose

Define how the application shall locate, calibrate, and track a physical
chessboard while chess pieces are present.

The subsystem shall:

1. Reconstruct the 8x8 playing surface from perspective-distorted camera
   images.
2. Avoid confusing the board's border, thickness, side faces, or shadow with
   the playing surface.
3. Freeze and wait while a hand or temporary obstruction prevents reliable
   measurement.
4. Perform camera-to-projector calibration without requiring an empty board.
5. Use only evidence belonging to the planar playing surface.
6. Preserve the last confirmed board geometry whenever current evidence is
   incomplete.
7. Provide the geometric and temporal foundation for later piece occupancy and
   chess-move detection.

## 2. Scope

This specification covers:

- Playing-surface localization from outer and partial internal evidence.
- Perspective-aware reconstruction of an equal-square 9x9 grid.
- Motion and obstruction gating.
- Structured-light calibration with pieces present.
- Separation of board-plane pixels from pieces and other non-planar objects.
- Confidence-aware local correction and validation.
- Stable board tracking during a game.
- Inputs required by occupancy and legal-move inference.

It does not initially require:

- Visual classification of every piece type.
- Reconstruction of an arbitrary chess position from one image.
- Calibration while a hand is moving over the board.
- Calibration when too little playing surface is visible.
- Automatic recalibration after camera or projector movement.

## 3. Physical and Geometric Assumptions

### 3.1 Playing surface

- The 64 physical squares have equal width and height.
- The playing surface is planar.
- The playing area forms an 8x8 square lattice with 9x9 intersections.
- The playing-area boundary may be inside a decorative border.
- The board may have visible thickness and vertical side faces.
- The board may be viewed with substantial perspective.
- The camera and projector may have smooth lens distortion.

Equal physical squares do not imply equal camera-image widths. Equal spacing
shall be evaluated in canonical board coordinates or after projective
rectification, never directly in the perspective-distorted image.

### 3.2 Pieces and other objects

- A normal starting arrangement is a required calibration scenario.
- Pieces are non-planar objects and may obscure square centers, corners, grid
  lines, and parts of the outer boundary.
- A hand or temporary object may obscure most of the board.
- Pieces may cast shadows and create highlights on adjacent squares.
- Projected light may illuminate a piece instead of the square beneath it.

### 3.3 Orientation

- Standard color orientation has a white bottom-right square when viewed from
  White's side.
- Square-color parity resolves odd 90-degree orientation errors.
- Square colors alone cannot distinguish 0 from 180 degrees.
- White-side identity shall later be resolved from known starting placement,
  piece colors, printed board evidence, or explicit user confirmation.

## 4. Coordinate Model

The canonical playing surface shall use board coordinates:

- Top-left playing-area corner: `(0, 0)`.
- Bottom-right playing-area corner: `(8, 8)`.
- Grid intersections: integer coordinates from 0 through 8.
- Square centers: half-integer coordinates such as `(0.5, 0.5)`.

A planar homography shall map canonical board coordinates to camera
coordinates. A separate calibrated mapping shall map camera-plane coordinates
to projector coordinates.

The 9x9 grid shall be generated by transforming the canonical equal-square
lattice. Image-space edges shall not be divided into eight equal pixel
lengths.

## 5. Playing-Surface Boundary

### 5.1 Boundary classes

The detector shall distinguish:

1. The boundary of the 8x8 playing surface.
2. The outer edge of any decorative top border.
3. The visible silhouette of the complete board.
4. Vertical side faces caused by board thickness.
5. Shadows and background edges.

The largest visible quadrilateral shall not automatically be treated as the
playing surface.

### 5.2 Required evidence

A playing-surface candidate shall combine as much of the following evidence as
is currently visible:

- Four top-plane boundary lines.
- Partial internal grid-line segments.
- Two perspective vanishing-point families.
- Equal-square geometry after rectification.
- Alternating square-color transitions.
- Temporal consistency with the previous confirmed board.
- Structured-light consistency with one planar homography.

A candidate shall not require every grid line, intersection, or square center
to be visible.

### 5.3 Perspective rectification

The detector shall:

1. Group visible line segments into the two board-grid directions.
2. Estimate their perspective vanishing points.
3. Fit a homography from the canonical square board to the playing plane.
4. Rectify the candidate board.
5. Evaluate grid spacing, square shape, and color alternation in the rectified
   image.
6. Map the accepted 9x9 lattice back to the camera image.

Opposite edges need not be parallel or equal in the camera image.

### 5.4 Board thickness rejection

Visible side faces shall be rejected when they:

- Fail the playing-plane homography.
- Do not belong to both board vanishing-point families.
- Produce nonuniform canonical square geometry after rectification.
- Disagree with extrapolated internal grid lines.
- Extend beyond the previously confirmed top playing plane without supporting
  planar evidence.

## 6. Temporal State Machine

The subsystem shall use explicit states:

1. `searching`
2. `candidate_accumulating`
3. `stable_board`
4. `motion_or_occlusion`
5. `candidate_moved_board`
6. `calibrating`
7. `validation`
8. `insufficient_evidence`

### 6.1 Searching

When no board geometry is known, candidates shall accumulate confidence across
multiple frames. A single quadrilateral shall never establish a board.

### 6.2 Stable board

The last confirmed geometry remains authoritative while normal play occurs.
Current detections may refine confidence but shall not introduce jitter.

### 6.3 Motion or occlusion

When significant foreground motion or loss of boundary support is present:

- Freeze the last confirmed board geometry.
- Do not recalibrate.
- Do not emit calibration errors.
- Do not interpret square changes as a chess move.
- Wait until motion has ended and evidence is stable again.

### 6.4 Moved-board candidate

A new board position may replace the old position only after:

- Motion has ended.
- A coherent planar transformation is measured.
- The candidate remains stable for several frames.
- Geometry is plausible and sufficiently supported.
- Any active projected-square evidence is consistent or verification is
  postponed.

## 7. Confidence and Update Policy

Confidence shall include:

- Boundary-line support.
- Internal-line support.
- Rectified equal-spacing error.
- Rectified square-shape error.
- Alternating-color consistency.
- Temporal stability.
- Distance from the previous confirmed geometry.
- Structured-light planar inlier ratio when available.

The system shall distinguish:

- **Confirmed:** safe to use or update.
- **Provisional:** collect more frames; do not update.
- **Occluded:** retain previous geometry silently.
- **Inconclusive:** retain previous calibration and explain why measurement
  cannot currently proceed.
- **Confirmed calibration error:** indicate camera or projector error but retain
  the last verified calibration until the user requests recalibration.

Absence of evidence is not evidence that the board moved.

## 8. Calibration with Pieces Present

### 8.1 Initial localization

Calibration may begin when:

- The top playing-plane boundary is confirmed.
- Motion has stopped.
- Enough of the playing plane is expected to remain visible.

An empty board shall not be required.

### 8.2 Structured-light capture

The existing Gray-code procedure may illuminate the complete board region.
Decoded camera-to-projector correspondences will include:

- Playing-surface pixels.
- Piece surfaces.
- Board borders and side faces.
- Shadows, reflections, and unreliable pixels.

### 8.3 Robust plane estimation

The calibration algorithm shall:

1. Decode all sufficiently modulated correspondences.
2. Fit a global playing-plane homography with robust RANSAC.
3. Classify homography-consistent correspondences as board-plane candidates.
4. Reject large residuals as non-planar or unreliable.
5. Refit using distributed board-plane inliers.
6. Verify that support covers the board rather than one local region.

The initial illumination check shall allow individual piece-obstructed squares
to respond weakly. It shall require at least 70% overall response and at least
35% average response in each board quadrant instead of requiring a fixed
minimum response in every square.

This robust fit breaks the dependency between knowing the piece mask and
knowing the board-plane mapping.

### 8.4 Board-plane visibility mask

The output shall include a confidence mask identifying:

- Reliable playing-plane support.
- Probable pieces or other above-plane objects.
- Saturated pixels.
- Underexposed or insufficiently modulated pixels.
- Unsupported areas.

The mask shall be retained for local correction, validation-target selection,
and diagnostics.

## 9. Local Correction with Occlusion

Each local correction-mesh node shall have:

- Measured correction.
- Number and distribution of supporting pixels.
- Confidence.
- Measured or interpolated status.

When a piece obscures a node:

- Interpolate from nearby reliable nodes.
- Preserve smoothness of the optical correction field.
- Reject calibration only when an unsupported region is too large for safe
  interpolation.

Piece surfaces shall never provide local board-plane correction samples.

## 10. Validation with Pieces Present

Fixed square-center validation is not sufficient because a piece may occupy a
target square.

For the known standard starting position, the implemented baseline uses four
internal corner-region grid intersections, four internal edge-region grid
intersections, and the board center. Quick realignment uses the corner-region
patches and center for fitting, then reserves the edge-region patches for
independent validation. This layout avoids the physical board edge and piece
centers while retaining broad board distribution. The outer target rows lie
at coordinates 2 and 6, bordering the unobstructed middle half rather than the
piece-filled gap between pawns and back-rank pieces. A missing target is
retried once at a nearby piece-clear location in the same region.

The validator shall:

1. Select visible, planar, high-confidence patches automatically.
2. Distribute targets across the board.
3. Avoid piece masks, side faces, grid boundaries, shadows, and saturation.
4. Reserve validation locations that were not used to fit the candidate.
5. Measure errors as a fraction of local square width.
6. Accept only when the configured maximum error and distribution requirements
   pass.

If insufficient validation patches are visible:

- Return `inconclusive`.
- Make the projector black.
- Preserve the previous verified calibration.
- Do not demand that every piece be removed.

## 11. Board Tracking During Play

### 11.1 Stationary board

The normal assumption is that the board does not move. The last confirmed
geometry shall remain stable without continuous replacement from noisy
detections.

### 11.2 Planar board movement

When the board moves within the calibrated plane:

- Detect a stable new playing-surface quadrilateral.
- Estimate the planar transformation from the old geometry.
- Transform the complete 9x9 grid.
- Keep camera-to-projector optical calibration.
- Verify later using visible planar evidence.

### 11.3 Non-planar movement

Board tilt, lifting, or a change of support height invalidates the old planar
relationship. Such movement shall be distinguished from planar translation
through geometric and projected-target evidence.

## 12. Piece Occupancy Interface

The localization subsystem shall expose:

- Confirmed 9x9 camera grid.
- Sixty-four camera-space square polygons.
- Playing-plane visibility/confidence mask.
- Motion/occlusion state.
- Stable-frame notification.
- Board-orientation parity.
- Geometry confidence and reason codes.

The first piece-analysis milestone shall classify each square as:

- Empty.
- Occupied.
- Unknown/occluded.

Piece identity recognition is not required for this milestone.

## 13. Move-Detection Foundation

After a stable pre-move and post-move position are available:

- Compare occupancy and appearance changes across all 64 squares.
- Generate legal moves from the known chess position.
- Score candidate legal moves against observed changes.
- Support captures, castling, en passant, and promotion.
- Ask for user confirmation when evidence is ambiguous.
- Never silently update game state from a low-confidence candidate.

## 14. Diagnostics

Each localization or calibration attempt should be able to save:

- Original camera frame.
- Motion mask.
- Playing-surface candidate quadrilateral.
- Rectified board view.
- Visible line segments and vanishing-point assignments.
- Reconstructed 9x9 grid.
- Square-color parity measurements.
- Structured-light plane residual map.
- Board-plane support mask.
- Piece/non-planar mask.
- Local-mesh support confidence.
- Selected validation patches.
- Confidence components and rejection reasons.

Diagnostics shall be bounded by the existing retention policy.

## 15. Failure and Safety Behavior

- Projector output shall be black on cancellation, inconclusive calibration, or
  rejected validation.
- Hands and temporary occlusions shall not produce modal errors.
- The system shall retain the previous confirmed geometry and verified
  calibration whenever new evidence is insufficient.
- No automatic full recalibration shall occur.
- Calibration updates shall be committed only after independent validation.
- A failed piece-aware attempt shall not overwrite a successful saved
  calibration.

## 16. Automated Tests

Synthetic tests shall cover:

- Perspective-distorted equal-square boards.
- Unequal image-space side lengths.
- Decorative borders and visible board thickness.
- Partial outer-edge occlusion.
- Missing internal grid segments.
- Piece-shaped non-planar masks.
- Hand-motion intervals followed by stable recovery.
- False rectangular background candidates.
- Homography fitting with structured-light outliers.
- Distributed versus locally concentrated plane support.
- Local-mesh interpolation across covered nodes.
- Dynamic selection of visible validation patches.
- Planar board translation.
- Board tilt or height changes.
- Color-parity orientation.

## 17. Hardware Acceptance Tests

The subsystem shall be tested with:

1. A normal complete starting position.
2. Several midgame positions with uneven piece distribution.
3. A hand crossing and temporarily covering most of the board.
4. Captured pieces near but outside the board.
5. The board translated within the tabletop plane.
6. The board tilted or lifted.
7. The camera moved slightly.
8. The projector moved slightly.
9. Strong piece shadows and moderate ambient-light changes.
10. The visible board side included in the camera image.

Acceptance requires:

- No empty-board prerequisite.
- No geometry update during hand motion.
- Correct 9x9 reconstruction after perspective rectification.
- Rejection of board-side and silhouette contamination.
- Distributed board-plane structured-light support.
- Independent validation on visible planar patches.
- Preservation of the previous calibration on inconclusive or failed attempts.

## 18. Implementation Milestones

### Milestone 1: Stable top-plane localization (implemented)

- Detect playing-surface boundary.
- Fit perspective-aware canonical grid.
- Reject border, thickness, and background rectangles.
- Add temporal confidence and motion freeze.

Automated acceptance covers a complete starting position, visible board
thickness, hand or scene motion, temporary missing detections, and stable
board movement. Real-hardware acceptance with the target board, camera, and
projector remains required.

### Milestone 2: Piece-aware structured-light plane

- Decode with pieces present. (Implemented)
- Fit robust planar correspondences. (Implemented)
- Produce an internal board-plane mask for local correction. (Implemented;
  persistence and diagnostics remain proposed)
- Measure distributed support before extrapolation. (Implemented)

### Milestone 3: Confidence-aware correction and validation

- Attach support confidence to local mesh nodes.
- Interpolate covered nodes.
- Select visible independent validation patches.
- Preserve old calibration on inconclusive results.

### Milestone 4: Stable occupancy

- Detect empty, occupied, and unknown squares.
- Wait through hand motion.
- Produce pre-move and post-move stable states.

### Milestone 5: Legal move inference

- Integrate chess position and legal-move generation.
- Score normal moves and captures.
- Add castling, en passant, promotion, and user confirmation.

## 19. Open Decisions

- Minimum number and spatial distribution of planar calibration inliers.
- Maximum unsupported local-mesh area.
- Required number and distribution of validation patches.
- Motion and stable-frame timing thresholds.
- Whether initial board-region selection needs optional user assistance.
- How White's side is established for arbitrary non-starting positions.
- Whether piece identity recognition is needed beyond legal-move inference.
